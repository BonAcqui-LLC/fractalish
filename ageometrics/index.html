Ageometrics

Ageometrics: Geometric Sufficiency and Residue Analysis measures how much target-relevant information a declared geometry retains, what remains outside it, and what must be added back.

What changed in v0.3. Version 0.3 formalizes GSR through Bayes-optimal risk, makes the empirical quantity explicitly protocol-dependent, distinguishes bounded canonical residue from signed empirical diagnostics, and narrows the novelty claim in light of skill-score and sufficiency-analysis precedents.

Comparator discipline is part of the definition. A GSR estimate without a declared comparator protocol is not an Ageometrics result.

Why geometry's success creates a blind spot

Geometry has become one of the most successful compressive languages in science and machine learning. Coordinates, manifolds, graphs, embeddings, topological summaries, and state spaces can organize astonishing amounts of structure. That success creates a risk: once nearly anything can be represented geometrically, the existence of a useful geometry stops proving that geometry is sufficient for causality, provenance, semantics, history, or identity.

What Ageometrics studies

Ageometrics asks a narrower question than "can this system be represented geometrically?" It asks what must be added back before a declared geometry becomes sufficient for a declared target, and how costly that restoration is.

  • Declared fuller reference record: the admissible richer record used for comparison, not a claim of metaphysical completeness.
  • Declared geometry: the tested representation, not geometry in the abstract.
  • Declared target: what the representation is being asked to predict, explain, or preserve.
  • Declared comparator protocol: the temporal, learner, capacity, loss, uncertainty, and leakage rules that make the comparison interpretable.

Non-Geometric Residue taxonomy

The residue taxonomy is a proposed working partition, not a settled ontology.

Relationship to Fractalish

Fractalish and Ageometrics remain paired rather than competing programs. Fractalish asks what history becomes visible in form. Ageometrics asks what history disappears when reality is reduced to form.

Blind growth and local contact

One strong motivation comes from blind or locally informed growth. In branching, aggregation, fracture, morphogenesis, and local-memory systems, the active front often lacks a representation of the completed whole. It responds to local contact, finite-range fields, and path-dependent consequences already embodied in the structure. That is why morphology can preserve partial history without preserving all of it.

Perceptual horizon

Perceptual horizon is the extent of information available to an updating process beyond its current local state. Varying it creates a practical way to test how much developmental history survives into final structure.

AI-memory application

Artificial memory is an especially strong benchmark because a machine can preserve much more of its own developmental record than a biological or geological specimen usually can. The comparison can therefore be measured directly: geometry versus full history, geometry plus provenance channel, geometry plus contradiction history, and so on.

Finance as a canonical test domain

Finance is useful because market research already leans heavily on visible geometry: price paths, volatility surfaces, fractal statistics, embeddings, and network structure. At the same time, the underlying mechanism can retain order-flow, depth, leverage, cancellation, and participant-history detail that the chart alone does not preserve. This remains a benchmark domain, not a trading claim.

Falsification and failure conditions

The program should weaken if the metric collapses into established skill-score or nested-model analysis without adding useful experiment design, if the residue taxonomy does not help diagnose failures, or if apparent residue vanishes under fair model-envelope testing.

Current research status

  • Defined: the field label, canonical population quantity, empirical estimator, and canonical versus empirical NGR distinction.
  • Proposed: representation-stability envelopes, encoding-cost frontier, perceptual-horizon experiments, and cross-domain residue recurrence.
  • Supported by neighboring literature: target-relative compression, skill-score normalization, causal insufficiency of observation alone, and mechanism-rich domains where visible form omits history.
  • Not yet established: exhaustive novelty, stable cross-domain comparability, and a final residue ontology.

Terminology note

The adjective ageometric already has an unrelated specialized use in geometric group theory. The present proposal concerns geometric sufficiency and residue analysis and does not use the term in that established sense.
