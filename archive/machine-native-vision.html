Machine-Native Vision

Machine-native vision asks what happens when sensing stops trying to imitate the human eye first.

Here, the goal is not simply to turn sensor data into a picture for a person. The goal is to detect, preserve, and emit morphology-bearing signals directly: branch patterns, fracture topology, erosion fronts, vascular structures, spatial roughness, and other forms that may function as process evidence.

This page belongs to the future-facing perception side of the stack. MCVA is about readout. Natural Math is about generation. Machine-native vision is about what kind of sensing substrate might feed those layers well.

Infinitysight belongs here as a future perception layer. It is about perception, not proof. A machine-native system may become better at seeing morphology across sensors and scales, but that does not mean it automatically knows what the morphology means.

For contributors, this is research rather than a shipping claim. It points toward sensing pipelines, robotics, inspection, multimodal morphology extraction, and machine-native tokenization while keeping unsolved components explicit.

The public release should keep this work aspirational and research-first.
