## V. THE FOUR TRIVIALIZATION CHECKS (MANDATORY, WITH GUARDS)

### 5.1 State trivialization — `R is just a state variable`
**Check:** For a native model S(t+1)=F(S,U), show that R is identical to a subset of S and adds zero predictive value once S is fully specified.

**Guard (against false trivialization):** You must actually specify S, and you must demonstrate it survives the **twin-state divergence test** (Event 003): two systems with identical measured S but different histories must NOT diverge under perturbation. If you cannot construct a Markovian S that passes twin-state divergence, the trivialization fails. If you claim "memory of shear is the fabric tensor," you must show the fabric tensor closes the dynamics at the task scale and fails the twin-state divergence nowhere.

### 5.2 Formalism trivialization — `the commonality is a representation artifact`
**Check:** Show the cross-domain similarity is created by shared mathematical representation (graph Laplacians, ODEs) rather than shared mechanism.

**Guard:** The Event 006 slime-mold/resistor finding is the model for a *valid* formalism trivialization (convergence entirely created by weighted-graph Laplacian representation). It is invalid if you merely note both systems have ODEs. You must show (a) no shared causal variable exists, and (b) the shared vocabulary carries no interventional information (blocking a central vein does not separate the models).

### 5.3 Physicalism trivialization — `it's just both being physical`
**Check:** Show the claimed "shared grammar" reduces to shared substrate constraints (dissipation, conservation).

**Guard:** Trivially, everything is physical. This check only bites if the candidate's specific relational structure (order, non-commutativity, distributional topology) adds nothing beyond the substrate law. "Stressed polymer and river delta both relax stress" is not a trivialization of the grammar; it is a trivialization only of a claim that grammar is physical substrate.

### 5.4 Universal-solvent / forbidden-set — `the grammar can accommodate any outcome`
**Check:** From the frozen operators alone, derive at least one concrete forbidden transition (a state transition that the grammar predicts cannot occur). Then attempt to find, in inspected literature, a system that exhibits that transition without the grammar's required write-process leaving a measurable R.

**Guard:** The forbidden prediction must be derived from the frozen operators + an explicit, pre-specified task/scale/observer index — not from a revised grammar. A failed forbidden-set prediction is a genuine falsifier (the Physarum calcium-wave counterexample in the prior audit is a legitimate data point). But you may not declare universal-solvent merely because the grammar could in principle be extended; you must show it was extended post hoc.
