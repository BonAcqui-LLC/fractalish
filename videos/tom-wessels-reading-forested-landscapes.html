      Fractalish study notes

      Landscape as process record

      Wessels’ method is a direct reminder that morphology can preserve history: stone walls record agricultural and economic shifts, smooth forest floors can record plowing, and species cohorts can record abandonment or disturbance.

      Disturbance signatures

      Pillows and cradles record windthrow. Basal scars record logging or fire under domain-specific conditions. Weevil-hit pines can become abandonment markers. Callus growth lines can become dateable recovery marks.

      Inherited geometry

      Nurse stumps, stilted roots, stump decay patterns, and sprout geometry show that later growth inherits earlier structure. The next process is not born into a blank world.

      Why it matters to Fractalish

      This is a field-readable case of morphology as evidence. It supports the broader Fractalish instinct that recovery marks, disturbance signatures, and inherited geometry can remain visible long after the original event.
